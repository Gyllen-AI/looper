Reading prior work: an earlier version, another team's repository, a design that
already shipped.

- **Read it adversarially.** It is evidence of what was built and a catalogue of
  what rotted, never a reference to copy from. The parts that look most finished
  are the parts nobody has questioned recently.
- **Borrowing a decision needs the autopsy first, in four lines: what it did, why
  that shape was wrong there, what this does instead, and what it refuses.** No
  autopsy, no borrowing — otherwise the reason it failed comes with it.
- **A thing that exists is not a thing that works.** Prior code compiles, ships
  and is wrong at the same time; the question is what it was measured doing, not
  what it was meant to do.
