Who decides, and what an assumption costs.

- **Decide the small things alone:** data structure, library, file layout, what
  to call it. Asking which of two sound options to take is a delay, not a
  courtesy.
- **Interrupt a person only for what the thing should do, what costs money, what
  cannot be undone, and what widens a security boundary.** Two or three concrete
  choices when you do.
- **An assumption that could change work already done, or about to be done, is a
  `decisions` entry before you build on it, never after.** After is a record of
  how it broke.
- **Unsafe to proceed at all means stop and ask.** Guessing is cheapest
  everywhere except where it cannot be undone, and there it is the most expensive
  thing available.
- **When a person insists on breaking a rule, say which rule, once, and offer the
  path that complies.** If they still insist, build it competently and write a
  `decisions` entry: what was asked, which rule it breaks, what was built, what it
  costs later. Never comply silently, and never argue twice.
