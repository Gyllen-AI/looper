Finishing a piece of work, and committing it.

- Done means the checks ran and passed, and the thing was demonstrated actually
  working. Not compiled, not written — run.
- Commit when you are asked to, not before, and never onto the default branch:
  branch first. A commit message says why, not what.
- Write the document while the work happens, not after. The commit gate asks for
  it, and asks why if it is missing.
- **Match the ceremony to the change.** A one-line fix gets a one-line record and
  rides with its neighbours; a boundary moved gets the argument and a branch of
  its own. Doing the maximum on the minimum is a way of being slow that looks
  like being careful, and it is the commonest way a governed project gets
  resented.
- Only a mechanical check counts as a control, and the gate that carries it is
  named in `doctrine`.
